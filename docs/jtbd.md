# Jobs To Be Done

**Wheely Arrival Companion** — independent concept study by Ayo Ahmed. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Wheely.

## Core jobs

### Passenger

1. **Orient** — *When I've just landed and I'm tired, help me see where I am in getting to my car, so I can stop wondering what happens next.*
   - Timeline of four self-reported stages: landed → passport control → baggage reclaim → meet chauffeur.

2. **Note a delay without effort** — *When I'm stuck in a queue, let me record it in one tap, so I don't have to compose anything while juggling luggage.*
   - Private delay check-in: preset reason, optional +10/+20/+30-minute rough estimate. Visible to the passenger only.

3. **Reassure on my terms** — *When someone is waiting on me, let me send one short reassurance — but only after I've seen the exact words and said yes.*
   - One-time, unchecked-by-default consent plus exact-text preview. Generic message. No reason, no time, no auto-share.

### Booker (synthetic "Morgan")

4. **Stop guessing** — *When the person I booked a car for has landed, I want a simple signal that they're moving — provided they chose to send it.*
   - A local simulation shows the current passenger-reported stage, with a generic delay line where applicable. It does not provide live data or transmit anything. Reason and estimate stay private. An explicitly approved stage summary is not described as private from its intended recipient.

## Context and forces

- The official product already tracks the flight, adjusts the pickup, shows the chauffeur on a map, offers passenger–chauffeur chat, and gives book-for-others recipients a tracking link with courtesy updates. These are facts, not gaps.
- The job this concept explores is different: the **passenger's own orientation** through arrivals, and **minimal-effort passenger-initiated** reassurance. Whether that job is real is an open question for testing.
- Lower post-landing attention is a hypothesis, not a researched finding. Keep controls simple; sharing deliberately requires review, consent and a separate action rather than a one-tap shortcut.

## Success looks like (proposed, no results yet)

- Passengers can identify the next step without feeling required to tap, check in or share. Completion is a mechanism metric, not sufficient evidence of usefulness.
- Delay check-ins take seconds, not sentences.
- No update is ever sent without explicit consent and preview — a hard requirement, not a metric to trade off.
